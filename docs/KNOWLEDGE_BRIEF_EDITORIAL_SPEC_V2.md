# Knowledge BRIEF Editorial Spec V2

Status: Draft for the paused Automation C quality review. This supplements V1 without changing the bytes of the policy pinned by existing semantic jobs.

## The reader's first screen

The first sentence of `lead` answers the question in `title` with a concrete judgment or action. The next two to four sentences explain when that judgment is needed in ordinary life. A reader arriving from search should not need the story, game, another article, or an agency document to understand it.

The public article follows these roles where relevant: direct answer; short real-world situation; visible decision criteria; application order; a clearly introduced example; a reusable checklist or record; the one or two safety boundaries that change the decision; evidence and its scope; then, for fiction-sourced questions, the brief final section `《생존일기》에서 시작한 질문`. The section role matters more than a fixed number of sections. Do not add empty or repetitive sections to fit a template.

## Evidence and examples

Write the practical explanation before naming an agency. Cite the agency where its finding supports the explanation. Before a water, power, transport, communications, or other narrow example, state the example's real-world conditions and why it illustrates the general question. Do not silently carry story conditions into a public claim. Narrative and personal experience are question provenance, not evidence. Keep every material claim within the existing Evidence claim, context, and limitation, or research and update Evidence before expanding the claim.

Group source notes in the existing `sources` list: what each source establishes and the scope in which the article uses it. Do not interrupt every paragraph with repeated jurisdiction or disclaimer language. Keep a short, precise warning at the point where the reader's action or safety judgment would change. HIGH risk, HUMAN_APPROVED, and review history cannot be lowered or inferred complete by prose editing.

## Submission checklist

Before submitting a new Candidate/Evidence/BRIEF package, explicitly check:

1. `candidate.question`, `evidence.question`, and `brief.title` are the same clear question.
2. The first sentence of `brief.lead` answers that question. A generic situation sentence or an agency introduction is insufficient.
3. The article stands alone when its final story/Reader section is removed.
4. The first domain-specific example states the situation before it uses the domain as proof.
5. Decision criteria or action order can be scanned in headings, a short list, or a table.
6. A fiction-sourced article places `《생존일기》에서 시작한 질문` last, with no reality claim based on the story. An experience-sourced article identifies the real experience accurately rather than relabeling it fiction.
7. Source notes, claim links, risk domains, publication policy, and the existing safety gate remain intact.

Machine checks may enforce structural facts, such as the final provenance section and a nonempty lead, but cannot establish that a Korean sentence actually answers the question or that a particular example has enough context. The Worker must make those editorial judgments and leave weak or uncertain packages in human review.
