Retrofit a real long-form survival/apocalypse spine onto the already-established continuous chronology without invalidating the genuine recovery, growth, property accumulation, relationships, and career progress already earned.

This is NOT a declaration that the previous peaceful period was secretly a preplanned calm before the storm. That history remains genuine. From the current point forward, however, the world has a hidden macro-arc so that play does not drift indefinitely into pure career/property/farming slice-of-life.

## Genre target from this point
Primary: long-form survival chronicle.
Secondary rewards: settlement growth, property, career, trade, competence, relationship, calm domestic life.

Desired rhythm:
`earned stability -> accumulating dependencies and things worth protecting -> external pressure -> costly adaptation -> partial recovery/growth -> qualitatively different pressure -> structural survival choices -> new world state`

Do not use constant disaster spam. Do not erase the pleasure of growth. But over the macro arc, survival pressure must eventually become existential at the level of livelihood, mobility, shelter, health, institutions, and/or human ties.

## Time scale
Target hidden horizon: roughly 2–4 additional in-world years, elastic.
- Time may compress during routine growth.
- Major acts may arrive earlier/later depending on player relocation, world outcomes, or accumulated state.
- Do not force exact calendar dates if current play makes them unnatural.

# MACRO ACTS

## ACT A — Consolidation With Real Value at Stake
Window: current winter 2027 into 2028, flexible.

Function:
- Let the current growth phase remain genuinely enjoyable.
- Establish the second rural house through actual use or deliberate non-use rather than immediately filling it because it exists.
- Continue career, money, property, agriculture, and Han relationship only where naturally earned.
- Show normal society functioning well enough that abandoning it is costly.

Survival pressure level: low-to-moderate, not zero.
Possible beats:
- seasonal cold, maintenance failures, insurance/repair costs, a transport disruption, local crop/input issue, illness/aging pressure, short grid or water faults.
- These must be real but not disguised as the main apocalypse.

Act question:
`What does Dohyun choose to maintain when he finally has more than he can personally operate?`

No mandatory permanent loss.

Transition conditions:
- at least one of company responsibility, second house, Han relationship, or urban/outer network has become meaningfully more valuable/entangled;
- enough peaceful time has passed that a larger external shock is not perceived as immediate GM correction.

## ACT B — Regional Physical Shock
Preferred window: 2028, seasonally plausible.

Core event class:
A severe regional weather/water disaster affecting the southeast and transport corridors — e.g. extraordinary monsoon/typhoon sequence with flooding, landslides, contaminated local water, road/bridge outages, agricultural damage, warehouse/industrial interruption.

This is seeded as a major external event, but exact track/intensity/local hit is NOT fixed until approached.

Why this event:
- It is qualitatively different from the original power/logistics outage.
- It directly tests land choice, drainage, road redundancy, water treatment, vehicles, agriculture, and the difference between urban and rural nodes.
- It can seriously damage infrastructure without requiring instant national collapse.

Do not guarantee:
- primary home survives untouched;
- second house survives untouched;
- 영수 truck survives;
- 우진 family remains in the same place;
- company/warehouse routes remain open.

But do not predetermine any loss. Use local exposure + prior preparation + player choices.

Open loss domains:
- access route / vehicle
- crops / stored goods / building damage
- temporary loss of one node
- injury or care burden
- company/logistics interruption

Act question:
`Can distributed resilience actually preserve options when geography itself becomes hostile?`

Success reward:
- preparedness should materially reduce casualties, contamination, displacement, and recovery time.
- a well-prepared player may preserve nearly everything, but only by making hard allocation choices under time pressure.

## ACT C — Reconstruction, Migration, and Unequal Recovery
Window: months after Act B.

Function:
Do NOT immediately follow disaster with another disaster. Give meaningful rebuilding and growth.

World effects may include:
- insurance disputes / repair cost spikes
- road/infrastructure rebuild priorities
- abandoned rural/industrial properties
- local migration and changed property values
- agricultural input shortages after physical damage
- consolidation of transport/logistics firms
- municipal resource prioritization
- more people seeking reliable local water, routes, storage, or rural footholds

This act is where success can become power/influence rather than only stockpile size.
Dohyun must not automatically become village leader. Influence can arise through assets, contracts, access, competence, trust, employment, or property without formal leadership.

Possible expansion:
- second house acquires a real role naturally
- a new independent person/family/business node may enter the network
- Han’s business may grow, diversify, or become exposed to debt/contract concentration
- company may benefit from competitors failing, creating both opportunity and dependence on Dohyun

Act question:
`What kind of life is being rebuilt, and who becomes dependent on whom?`

Danger:
This act must not drift indefinitely into pure healing/growth. Compress routine months once the new structure is clear.

## ACT D — East Asian Strategic / Maritime Supply Shock
Preferred window: 2029-ish, elastic; do not surface countdown.

Core event class:
A serious East Asian geopolitical/maritime crisis that disrupts sea lanes, fuel, industrial imports, shipping insurance, electronics/parts, and national security posture. The exact named conflict is not required far in advance; choose the most believable fictionalized regional configuration when the act approaches.

Default design:
- Korea is initially affected economically/logistically before direct kinetic danger.
- fuel allocation, strategic industry priority, freight restrictions, cyber/communications instability, financial controls, reserve mobilization, and shipping scarcity progressively matter.
- company and Han’s transport business become high-value/high-risk nodes rather than merely sources of income.
- rural food/water capacity matters more, but farming inputs/fuel/parts also become harder.

Escalation ladder (do not automatically climb all levels):
1. maritime/insurance disruption
2. import/fuel rationing and industrial prioritization
3. cyber/communications or financial interruptions
4. civil-defense/mobilization pressure and transport controls
5. limited direct security incidents affecting Korean territory or infrastructure IF world logic and prior escalation justify it

Player choices and world state can slow, reroute, or localize consequences. Do not force Level 5 solely for drama.

Open loss domains:
- job/company survival or role
- Han’s business / vehicles / contracts / personal mobility
- urban apartment access and city viability
- fuel and vehicle freedom
- legal/financial liquidity
- separation between people in different nodes

Act question:
`When the national system still exists but prioritizes survival over convenience, which parts of Dohyun’s old normal life remain compatible with his survival network?`

## ACT E — Compound National Survival Crisis
Window: only after Act D has accumulated enough strain; may be 2029–2030+.

Purpose:
This is the true apocalypse-threshold act for this worldline: not necessarily total state collapse, but a period where multiple critical systems cannot all be counted on at once.

Seeded compound pressures, activate only 2–3 at once:
- prolonged fuel/energy rationing and recurrent grid instability
- inflation / payment / bank withdrawal or capital controls
- medicine and hospital bottlenecks
- internal displacement from damaged/strategically sensitive regions
- agricultural input and food-price shock
- labor shortages / mobilization / transport permits
- local theft, black-market activity, and selective policing overload
- extreme seasonal weather acting on already-depleted systems

Important:
The apocalypse feeling comes from persistent constraint and shrinking fallback layers, not instant nationwide anarchy.
Police, government, hospitals, companies, and utilities may still function unevenly.

This act must force at least one genuinely irreversible strategic choice if world conditions support it, such as:
- which node remains primary when two cannot both be maintained
- whether to preserve corporate status or full-time outer-base operation
- whether to move/merge/separate living arrangements
- whether to commit the second house to people, trade, storage, or keep it vacant reserve
- whether to spend accumulated capital to preserve mobility/energy/water/medical access
- whether a relationship becomes shared-life commitment, geographic separation, or remains independent

No mandatory death.
No guaranteed preservation.
No arbitrary raid as a shortcut.

Act question:
`What survives when Dohyun finally has more important things than he can protect simultaneously?`

## ACT F — New Order / Long Conclusion
Window: after the compound crisis reaches a durable state.

Do not preselect one ending.
Potential world-state families:
1. constrained but functioning national recovery — stronger regulation, scarcer resources, new normal
2. uneven regional recovery — cities/regions diverge sharply; local resilient nodes matter
3. prolonged national decline — institutions persist but household/local networks carry much more burden
4. partial regional collapse / relocation — one existing living zone becomes nonviable and the chronology continues elsewhere

Character ending is generated from play, not selected from a fixed menu.
The chronicle may continue beyond this macro spine if the user wants.

Act question:
`After years of surviving and building, what did Dohyun actually create: property, a network, a household, a livelihood, influence, or a place that others can survive through?`

# PRESSURE DISCIPLINE

## Maximum active fronts
Keep max 3 major fronts, usually 1 dominant + 1–2 secondary.
Never dump the entire macro spine into the foreground.

## Foreshadowing rule
Foreshadow only what the current world could plausibly reveal:
- weather forecasts and hydrology before physical weather event
- commodity/shipping/insurance/news/business signals before maritime crisis
- rationing, contracts, shortages, government notices as strategic pressure grows

Do not use ominous narration that tells the player an act is coming.

## No retroactive prophecy
Previous recovery was real.
The world is not secretly destined to collapse because the player succeeded.
Future events begin from new causes developing after the current state.

## Player agency
Macro event classes are seeded; exact local outcomes remain open.
The player may:
- avoid direct exposure
- preserve people/assets through preparation
- abandon an asset before loss
- change region
- change employment/business position
- build new relationships
- cause a planned pressure to hit a different weakness than expected

If player action makes an exact prepared scene impossible, discard the scene and preserve only the macro cause.

## Loss fairness
For permanent loss require believable cause and at least one of:
- prior warning/signals
- time-pressure tradeoff
- resource insufficiency
- accepted risk
- unavoidable external scale beyond player capacity

Do not make every careful decision succeed, but never erase preparation just to maintain difficulty.

## Relationship rule
한지연 is important but not plot-armored and not designated tragedy bait.
No prewritten death/separation/romance ending.
Her independent business, parents, goals, and geographic choices must remain real sources of divergence.
Same for 영수, 우진, 민석, 선희, and future people.

## Growth rule
Allow genuine victories after each crisis:
- better property use
- new equipment
- deeper trust
- business/career advantage
- production capability
- new people/nodes

The next crisis should test different dependencies, not simply scale the same problem upward.
